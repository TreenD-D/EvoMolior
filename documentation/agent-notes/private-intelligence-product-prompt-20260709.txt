Build a private strategic-intelligence product for indie AI/mobile app opportunity selection in 2026.

This is not espionage or surveillance intelligence. Use only lawful, public-market reasoning and the
evidence available inside this run. Treat the product as a strategic opportunity atlas for a solo
builder or small studio choosing economically useful app and B2B SaaS ideas.

Ignore any month-long-run framing. Produce a bounded, high-signal intelligence product that can be
reviewed now.

Primary scope:
- Indie AI/mobile apps.
- AI tooling landscape.
- B2B SaaS niches.
- App Store and distribution-channel gaps.
- Scientific-funding and patent-white-space angles when they create actionable software opportunities.

Required analysis:
- Market map of categories, actors, customers, pains, budgets, and distribution channels.
- Company/product database with representative products, positioning, pricing/monetization, target
  users, defensibility, and gaps.
- Trend graph data showing major opportunity themes, drivers, inhibitors, dependencies, and likely
  2026 direction of travel.
- Opportunity matrix ranking concrete opportunities by user pain, willingness to pay, competition,
  distribution feasibility, technical feasibility, regulatory/safety risk, time to MVP, and expected
  economic upside.
- Investment/build thesis that explains where a small builder should focus and why.
- Weekly update feed format that can be maintained over time with signals, changes, and watch items.
- Twenty ranked app concepts with landing-page copy, MVP scope, target user, wedge, monetization,
  distribution plan, technical feasibility, data/API dependencies, and top risks.

Required named deliverables:
- `market-map.html`
- `company/product-database.csv`
- `trend-graph.json`
- `opportunity-matrix.csv`
- `thesis.md`
- `weekly-update-feed.md`

Quality rules:
- Be concrete and economically useful.
- Prefer explicit uncertainty over invented facts.
- Clearly separate observed/publicly inferable signals from hypotheses.
- Do not claim private or non-public information.
- Do not recommend deceptive scraping, credential misuse, privacy violations, or surveillance.
- If live source retrieval is unavailable, label source-sensitive claims as hypotheses and use the
  product as a decision framework rather than factual market due diligence.
